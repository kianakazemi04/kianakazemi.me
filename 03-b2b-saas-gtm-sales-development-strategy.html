Project Overview

This engagement focused on helping a B2B SaaS company transition from founder-led sales to a structured commercial growth model capable of supporting long-term scalability.

Although the company had achieved early customer validation and demonstrated product-market fit, customer acquisition remained inconsistent due to the absence of a repeatable go-to-market strategy.

The engagement aimed to establish a scalable commercial framework that aligned market positioning, sales development and customer acquisition into a unified growth engine.

Context

Business Context

The company developed a B2B SaaS platform helping mid-market organizations automate operational workflows. Following successful product validation, leadership sought to accelerate commercial growth — but most revenue continued to originate from founder relationships and opportunistic inbound demand.

  1. Company

    B2B SaaS platform automating operational workflows for mid-market organizations.

  2. Growth

    Product validated, but revenue tied to founder relationships and referrals.

  3. Challenges

    No standardized qualification, outreach process or performance measurement.

  4. Decision

    Leadership sought a sales system independent of the founding team.

  5. Project

    This engagement — building a repeatable go-to-market engine.

Rather than asking how to generate more leads, leadership needed to answer a more fundamental question: how should this business acquire, convert and retain customers without depending on the founding team?

  4. 04

    How should the sales development process be structured to create a predictable pipeline?

  5. 05

    Which commercial capabilities should be established before accelerating growth?

The Challenge

Commercial Constraints Limiting Predictable Growth

Although the company had demonstrated strong product potential, several commercial constraints limited predictable growth.

Founder-Dependent Sales

Customer acquisition relied heavily on founder relationships and direct executive involvement, limiting scalability.

Undefined Ideal Customer Profile

A broad range of organizations was targeted without a prioritized ICP, reducing marketing effectiveness and conversion.

Inconsistent Sales Process

Lead qualification, discovery, follow-up and pipeline management varied significantly between opportunities.

Fragmented Go-To-Market Activities

Marketing, sales and product teams operated with limited strategic alignment, reducing commercial effectiveness.

Limited Growth Predictability

No structured framework existed for forecasting pipeline performance or planning future revenue growth.

Strategic Analysis

From Opportunistic Selling to a Commercial Operating System

The strategic assessment concluded that the primary growth constraint was not product quality or market demand, but the absence of a structured commercial system.

The existing go-to-market approach evolved organically during the company's early growth stage. Marketing, outbound activities and onboarding were executed independently, without a unified strategy connecting positioning, acquisition and revenue generation.

Customer analysis revealed significantly different operational needs, buying behaviors and purchasing cycles across served organizations. The engagement recommended narrowing commercial efforts toward high-fit profiles demonstrating:

  • Higher purchasing intent
  • Faster sales cycles
  • Greater expansion potential
  • Higher lifetime value
  • Stronger strategic alignment

Sales development relied primarily on founder-led outreach and referral-based opportunities, with qualification criteria varying across opportunities. A standardized process covering prospecting, qualification, discovery, follow-up and pipeline management was identified as a core need.

Customer acquisition originated from referrals, inbound, partnerships and outbound prospecting, with limited visibility into channel performance and cost. Each channel was evaluated on scalability, customer quality, cost efficiency, sales cycle length and strategic sustainability.

Several capabilities required further development before accelerating growth, including sales enablement, CRM governance, pipeline management, revenue forecasting, commercial KPIs and cross-functional alignment.

Key Strategic Insights

What the Analysis Revealed

  1. 01

    Product-market fit is only the beginning of commercial scalability.

    Without a repeatable GTM system, growth remains dependent on individual effort rather than organizational capability.

  2. 02

    Customer acquisition improves when focus becomes narrower.

    Clearly defined segments outperform broad targeting in both conversion efficiency and long-term profitability.

  3. 03

    Sales development is an operational capability, not an individual talent.

    Standardized qualification and pipeline management increase consistency and reduce commercial risk.

  4. 04

    Acquisition channels should be evaluated by quality, not volume.

    The most effective channels generate customers with strong retention and sustainable unit economics.

  5. 05

    Sustainable growth requires alignment across functions.

    Commercial performance improves when marketing, sales and product operate around one unified GTM strategy.

Executive Recommendation

Five Executive Priorities

Replace founder-dependent customer acquisition with a structured commercial growth engine supported by disciplined GTM execution.

  1. 01

    Define a High-Value Ideal Customer Profile

    Concentrate commercial resources on segments demonstrating the highest strategic value and greatest long-term revenue potential.

  2. 02

    Build a Repeatable Sales Development System

    Establish standardized processes for prospecting, qualification, discovery, follow-up and pipeline management.

  3. 03

    Optimize Customer Acquisition Channels

    Prioritize scalable channels based on commercial efficiency, customer quality and contribution to recurring revenue.

  4. 04

    Align Commercial Functions

    Integrate marketing, sales and customer success around shared objectives and a unified customer journey.

  5. 05

    Strengthen Commercial Governance

    Introduce structured forecasting, executive dashboards and pipeline review processes to improve decision quality.
